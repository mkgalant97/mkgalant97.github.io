M.S. Thesis · Atmospheric Sciences

Evaluating Evaporation from Land Surfaces near the Great Salt Lake

University of Utah · August 2025 · Advisor: Dr. John D. Horel · Committee: Dr. Sebastian Hoch, Dr. Kevin Perry

This pilot study investigated momentum, sensible, and latent heat fluxes over two surface types (mineral ponds and exposed playa) within the GSL’s meander line using two different eddy covariance sensor systems (Campbell Scientific EC150 and the LI-COR LI-710). The primary goal was to assess sensor performance and evaluate the feasibility of using the less expensive LI-710 as a substitute for the EC150 for long-term monitoring of evaporative fluxes in the harsh environments within the GSL Basin.

Read the full thesis (PDF)

Abstract

The desiccation of saline lakes, such as the Great Salt Lake (GSL), presents significant environmental challenges, including increased water insecurity and habitat destruction. This phenomenon is partially dependent on natural variability and climate change, but is largely influenced by excessive water use and management policy. An essential component of water balance within closed lake systems is the amount that is lost to evaporation and evapotranspiration. These processes within the GSL basin have been incompletely studied.

This study evaluates evaporation fluxes from both exposed playa and anthropogenic mineral pond surfaces using two eddy covariance sensor systems: the open-path, three-dimensional Campbell Scientific EC150 and the semi-open-path, one-dimensional LI-COR LI-710. LI-COR’s release of the lower cost LI-710 during 2024 provided the impetus for comparing its performance characteristics to those of the EC150. Analyses are conducted of daily evaporation rates, energy flux patterns, and sensor performance under distinct environmental conditions. Instruments were deployed at a playa site in Farmington Bay and at a mineral pond site operated by Compass Minerals, with data evaluated in this study from October 2024 through March 2025. The procedures used to assess sensor performance will be discussed in detail, using data collected at these sites

Differences in flux rates between the playa and mineral pond sites highlight the impact of surface type, moisture availability, and atmospheric dynamics and thermodynamics on evaporation processes. The spatial heterogeneity of these surface types within the GSL boundary further underscores the importance of localized measurements for accurate quantification. Preliminary results indicate that the LI-710 systematically underestimates evaporation rates compared to the EC150. Feedback from LI-COR staff suggests that this underestimation is recognized and requires an LI-710 sensor firmware upgrade that will be made available during summer 2025.

Key findings

  1. 01

    Diagnostic Code Utility

    The diagnostic flags from both sensor systems proved very useful for evaluating potential sensor performance issues. The EC150's QC flag system, based on turbulence characteristics and stationarity tests (Foken et al., 2004, 2012), identified 30-minute samples of questionable flux measurements, particularly during weak or intermittent turbulence common during stable periods during the cool season, particularly at the UUPYF site. Of less concern due to appropriate sensor siting is the relatively infrequent periods (<10%) when the wind direction was towards or away from the 3-D sensors’ booms. The LI-710’s diagnostic codes also flagged performance issues, particularly when the internal cell temperature dropped below 5C or when airflow was obstructed.

  2. 02

    Environmental Conditions and Site Differences

    Data quality and sensor performance varied notably between the two sites due to differences in surface characteristics and atmospheric conditions. At UUPYF, the playa surface is a frequently changing environment that undergoes substantial seasonal transitions: it can be flooded, frozen, or snow-covered during winter, flooded in spring, and typically dries out during summer and fall (Hang et al., 2015; Massey et al., 2017). This seasonal variability results in larger diurnal temperature swings and more frequent periods of boundary layer stability, particularly during calm, cold nights in winter when turbulence diminishes. These stable conditions complicate flux measurements, as eddy covariance techniques rely on turbulent mixing to accurately estimate vertical fluxes (Foken et al., 2012a). Very stable conditions (ζ ≥ 0.4) occurred during 9% of the cool season at UUPYF, more than double that observed at UUCMF. Likewise, very unstable conditions (ζ ≤ −0.4) were more common at UUPYF (16%) than UUCMF (10%), reflecting the playa’s wider diurnal range and flux responses to variable surface conditions.

  3. 03

    Sensor Performance Discrepancies

    Consultation with LI-COR staff provided a possible explanation for the observed underestimation of LE by the LI-710 relative to the EC150. Such underestimates have also been reported by other researchers comparing the LI-710 to fully three-dimensional eddy covariance systems (Peddinti and Kisekka, 2024). That the LI-710’s one-dimensional estimates of H generally agree well with the EC150’s three-dimensional measurements points toward an issue specific to the water vapor pathway.

  4. 04

    Estimating Evaporation

    While the focus of the pilot study is to provide recommendations on ways to measure evaporation near the Great Salt Lake, preliminary daily evaporation estimates can be provided from the EC-150 sensors for the cool season. Current evaporation estimates derived from this study, conducted during the cool season are 0.52 mm day⁻¹ for playa and 0.53 mm day⁻¹ for mineral ponds. Since these values are obtained after excluding substantive numbers of samples when evaporation may be small but cumulatively non-negligible, actual estimates may be higher. It is anticipated that less data will be excluded during the warm season (April-September) such that the estimates for the mineral pond and playa sites will be potentially more accurate for comparison to earlier studies. Prior estimates show a yearly average of 0.45-0.7 mm day-1 from the playa (Malek, 2003; Hang et al., 2015) and 3-5mm day-1 from mineral ponds (Turk, 1970).

Key figures

Future work

This pilot study has laid the groundwork for evaluating lower-cost eddy covariance sensor alternatives in the GSL basin, but many questions remain – particularly regarding the representativeness, reliability, and usability of evaporation estimates across all surface conditions and seasons. This research contributes to the broader GSL Basin Integrated Plan (Bingham et al., 2023) to help improve future monitoring strategies that inform water resource management policies in the face of ongoing lake desiccation and water scarcity challenges (Wurtsbaugh et al., 2017; Perry, 2024).

A firmware upgrade from LI-COR in summer 2025 will be evaluated through the remainder of the pilot study to assess whether the LI-710 underestimate of LE and evaporation rate is overcome. Additionally, expanding monitoring to other land cover types within the GSL Basin, including open water areas will be critical for basin-wide evaporation estimates (Lobos-Roco et al., 2022; Gu et al., 2024).
